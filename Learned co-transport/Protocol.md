# Cross-play among learned co-transport policies

## Research question

Does learning with a rotating population of trainable teammates improve cross-play on unfamiliar payload routes? Does using the last realised partner force and publicly observed failed alignment help independently? Earlier Bayesian pilots fixed the teammate policies. Here both robot policies learn from the shared transport reward, and the test pairs each focal policy with policies from independent training runs. The environment represents planar thrust and twist with discrete states. It excludes rigid-body simulation, humans and physical hardware. [Chu’s cooperative-agents project](https://www.findaphd.com/phds/project/multi-agent-reinforcement-learning-for-embodied-cooperative-agents/?p197574) · [Other-Play](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/hu20a.html) · [Behavior Diversity Play](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/szot23a.html)

## Co-transport task

Two robots grasp opposite ends of a payload as it passes five bends. The bend requires a hidden lateral push direction. Each robot sees its own independently noisy guide cue, local twist in its own reference frame and a rough/smooth support indicator. Each chooses left thrust, coast or right thrust. Nonzero thrust may slip. Opposing imbalances rotate the payload, balanced forces relax twist by one unit, and enough accumulated twist jams the payload. Two correctly aligned nonzero pushes advance a bend if the payload is stable. An incorrect aligned attempt at a stable payload gives a publicly observed failed alignment with a known attempted direction. The realised partner force can be sensed after acting. The environment advances on a joint action only.

The shared training reward gives +1 per bend, +1.5 upon completion, a base −0.06 each contact, −0.08 per remaining unit of twist, −0.70 for attempting the wrong bend, and −2.20 on a jam. A route ends at completion, jam or 22 contacts. The short-history state incorporates current cue, support and twist. The longer history adds the last realised partner force at the current bend, the previous own cue and any public failed alignment at that bend. Observations and rewards are identical across corresponding partner-training conditions. States encode actions and twist relative to the agent’s own cue. This supplies the same reflection equivariance to all learning arms.

## Training and comparators

Compare four decentralized tabular Q-learning arms in a 2 × 2 design: fixed pairs or rotating partners, crossed with short or longer observation history. Each arm uses 12 independently randomised training populations, each with eight learning policies per role. Each policy receives exactly 14,400 local transition updates, so each arm uses 1,382,400 joint transitions. All policies have matching learning rates, discounts and exploratory schedules. A rotating population redraws a permutation of partners every 24 transition steps and resets routes at the redraw. Fixed pairs keep their partners and perform identical resets. Within training blocks the simulator starts a fresh route on termination. An action uses only the acting policy’s observation and table at execution. Each actor updates its own table using its received scalar shared reward and its own next observation. A Bayesian guide-cue integrator with a twist-aware coast rule serves as an extra non-learning benchmark.

Two evaluation partner banks are trained separately from all four focal arms, using fresh disjoint seeds and difficult training variants. The first bank uses fixed pairs, longer history, higher guide noise and more rough support. The second uses rotating pairs, longer history, more persistent layouts and a stronger jam penalty during its own training. Both learn with the same number of local transitions as focal policies. Evaluation uses the common task reward for every method.

## Held-out evaluation and decision rule

Each of the 12 independent blocks uses its own trained focal and opponent populations. Evaluate both focal roles on 128 fresh routes per role, per panel. Each block includes the eight independently trained focal identities per role, with 16 different routes per identity. Across corresponding arms the cue/slip random numbers, starting routes and frozen opponent identities are matched. The two panels cross different fresh layouts/noise profiles with the two independently trained opponent banks. One has alternating bends and rougher support. The other has more persistent bends, poorer guide cues and rough-contact slip. Outcomes within a route can diverge as actions differ. Report route completion, jam rate, bends progressed, contact count and net reward. Also report training and same-population test diagnostics to show whether policies learned any coordination.

Co-primary contrasts are rotating versus fixed training with longer histories in each panel. The primary claim requires at least +0.03 completion probability in each panel and a positive one-sided Bonferroni lower confidence bound in each. Resample the 12 independent training/evaluation blocks with 10,000 paired cluster bootstrap draws. Report each comparison and its ordinary two-sided 95% interval, each rotation effect at shorter history, the memory contrasts within each pairing schedule, and the Bayesian guide baseline. Report negative results as recorded. No policy selection or parameter tuning uses these test panels. Separate training and test random seeds are committed in the implementation before test.
