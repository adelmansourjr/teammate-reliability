# Separating teammate convention from reliability

## Question

An agent can infer a new teammate's preferred direction without knowing how reliably the teammate can execute it. Does sharing a partner-specific reliability estimate across contact modes improve costly cooperative decisions after prior probe choice is held fixed? This follows the earlier shared-load pilot, where a route-frequency probe was as good as a more elaborate probe selector. Here the manipulated factor is the forecast and update rule after that same probe. This is a finite cooperative decision model with learned teammate forecasts, not training two embodied RL agents. [Earlier pilot](../Coordination%20probes/README.md)

Research on ad hoc teamwork treats a teammate's latent behaviour as a belief to update during cooperation. Embodied coordination studies motivate evaluation against teammates with behaviour outside the training population. Here the mathematical separation is between a convention by contact mode and execution lapse rates by support condition. [Bayesian type-based ad hoc teamwork](https://agents-lab.org/pub/) · [Behavior Diversity Play](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/szot23a.html) · [Other-Play](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/hu20a.html)

## Task and common budgets

A route has 24 contacts, three contact modes and two observed support conditions. At every contact the focal agent can apply either binary direction or abstain. Acting with the partner's realised direction earns +1, acting against it loses a visible penalty drawn uniformly from 0.7 to 4.6, and abstention earns zero. The partner's preference is a fixed vector of three directions. Execution flips that preference with a probability dependent on the partner and support condition. The agent sees a small imperfect cue about the preference at every contact, with 28% symmetric noise. It learns the realised force after its own push from the signed outcome. Abstaining reveals no force. All methods use exactly one up-front probe at the most frequent upcoming contact mode. It observes preferred direction with 15% error and costs 0.08 reward. They receive the same route, random cues, probe and latent outcomes. They cannot observe a partner identifier or the realised force before acting. Each policy maximises expected immediate reward under its forecast. This holds action selection fixed and leaves dual-control or look-ahead planning outside the comparison.

All deployable models are fitted from a common synthetic calibration population of 2,048 partners, each with 64 labelled execution trials per support condition. The preferred directions are known in this calibration fixture. Empirical type priors with half-count smoothing populate a fixed grid of slip rates from 0.04 to 0.46 in steps of 0.06. At test time the learner observes only actions and their outcomes. Training preferences have independent marginal probabilities (0.50, 0.30, 0.16), and slip rates share a beta-distributed reliability factor plus condition-specific noise. Routes are sampled from a shared heterogeneous Dirichlet mixture. No model is fitted or selected from test outcomes.

- Fixed population lapse, estimated from the calibration data.
- Fixed support-condition lapse averages, controlling for observed difficulty without learning a personal rate.
- Adaptive personal but support-agnostic lapse, with a calibration-fitted discrete prior and online Bayesian updates.
- Mode-local adaptive belief, with the full support-conditioned calibration prior but feedback shared only within a contact mode.
- Factorized support-adaptive belief, with the same support-specific lapse marginals as the full learner and no correlation assumed across them.
- Joint support-adaptive belief, with a learned empirical joint slip prior shared across modes.
- Preference oracle, reliability oracle, and dual oracle, with access to the actual preference, actual slip rates, or both. These use privileged information and are diagnostic comparisons, with probe costs retained.

## Held-out panels and analysis

Generate three distinct families with fresh seeds. Each consists of 256 independent partners and 20 matched routes per partner. The first has continuously distributed stationary rates from two distinct reliability regimes and correlated preferences. The second has a new source of cross-mode dependence and reversed support patterns for some partners, with continuous stationary rates. The third uses the second family's generator but may swap its rates and increase one at contact 13. The model is never notified. This third family tests temporal misspecification and is reported separately. The initial two are the co-primary stationary panels. They are distributions of simulated partners, not samples of human or real robot teammates.

Compare net reward per route. The preregistered primary fit test requires the joint learner to exceed fixed support, personal pooled lapse, and mode-local adaptation in each stationary panel. For practical relevance require +0.20 reward per 24-contact route per contrast. Compute 10,000 paired cluster bootstrap draws over independent partners. For the six primary lower bounds use one-sided Bonferroni percentile cutoffs at 0.05/6. Report ordinary two-sided 95% intervals on every contrast, including joint versus factorized, plus absolute mistimed pushes, matches, abstentions and Brier loss. Report first and second half reward and Brier loss to distinguish online learning from a stronger prior. Publish all results, including any shortfall. The outcome uses the prespecified fitted model, independently seeded panels and a frozen action rule. Sampling is paired at each contact even when action choices cause the policy's own observed history to diverge.
